## Do you have to clean out the house before you sell it?

Clearing the house out is your choice, and it does not change the disclosure rules that apply to your sale. What matters is what a cleanout gets you, and whether you can actually do it. For a lot of families the house feels too heavy to start on. That is a fair place to be, and it does not mean the house cannot be sold.

### What a cleanout does change

- It shows the real condition. Once the rooms are clear, you and any buyer can see the floors, walls, roof lines and anything that was hidden behind the belongings.
- It lets you decide with facts. A professional cleanout crew, then an inspection, tells you what you are really selling before you pick a route.
- It makes showing the home possible, which matters if you want to market it broadly.

### What a cleanout does not change

Clearing the house does not turn the disclosure paperwork into something optional. The disclosure rules apply to a sale of single-family residential property, except as the law provides. The disclosures are made on a copy of the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form. A waiver of the requirements is void as against public policy. So do not treat a cleanout as a way to avoid the form, and do not agree with anyone to skip it.

I am not telling you what must go on the form for your house, or whether an exception fits your sale. Go through it with a listing agent or a real estate attorney.

### When you might choose not to clear it first

- You do not have the time, health or help to run a cleanout and the repairs that may follow.
- The person living there is not ready to move, and a rushed cleanout would hurt them more than it helps the sale.
- The family has not settled who decides. Clearing out belongings before that is settled can start a fight that is hard to undo.
- You want privacy and fewer showings, and you accept that a buyer who takes the house with its condition will price that in.

None of those is a wrong answer. Each one trades something: time, price, control or certainty. Work out what you need most before you spend money on either path.

## Should you clear it out, list it, keep it or sell directly?

A full house and a family that has to decide what to do with it is a hard place to start. There is no single best route. What decides it is how much time you have, how much money can go into the house, and who has the right to decide. Each route below fits some situations and not others, and several have nothing to do with Twin Home Buyer.

### Clear it out first, then test the open market

This fits when you have the time and the money to clear the house and handle repairs, and you want to see what the open market offers afterward. A professional cleanout crew, followed by an inspection, can show you the real condition of the house before you list. The cost is time and cash up front, before you know what the house will bring. If you are handling a parent's home, [this post on selling a house full of belongings](https://www.twinhomebuyer.com/sell-bay-area-house-full-of-belongings/) covers the family side of clearing out.

### List it with an agent

This fits when the home can be cleared and shown, and you want to market it broadly. An agent who knows difficult-condition homes is worth asking about. Your part is the disclosure paperwork, so go through the form with your agent or an attorney.

Here is what the law says. The California disclosure article applies, except as provided in Civil Code 1102.2, to a transfer by sale of single-family residential property. The required disclosures are made on a copy of the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form. Any waiver of those requirements is void as against public policy, so there is no agreeing your way around it.

### Keep it for now

This fits when you are not under time pressure, the person living there is not ready to move, or the family still has to sort out who decides. Think of a family that needs time to arrange care for a parent before anyone talks about a sale. Keeping the home while care, cleanup and decisions get worked out can be better than a rushed sale. The cost is that the house keeps needing attention while you wait.

### Sell directly to a buyer

This fits when you want to avoid cleanout, repairs and showings, or you need privacy, and you accept that a direct buyer prices the condition of the house into the offer. That is the tradeoff. You give up some of what a broad open-market sale might bring, and in return you take on less work. Before you pick any buyer, ask what exactly they will and will not handle, and get it in writing. For a plain explanation of the term, see [what selling a house as-is means](https://www.twinhomebuyer.com/what-does-selling-your-house-as-is-mean/). Once you have weighed the other routes, you can [read how a direct sale of a packed or hoarded home works](https://www.twinhomebuyer.com/hoarder-houses/) as an optional next step.

### Get professional help before any of them

This is less a route than a step before any route. An attorney can speak to authority, probate and disclosure questions. A title company can speak to liens and title. A CPA can speak to taxes. The city or county can tell you whether any enforcement action exists on the property. Asking these questions early can keep more of your options open.

To choose, I'd start with four questions. How much time do you have? How much money can go into the house before it sells? Who has the authority to sign? How much of the work can your family carry? Your answers point to a route, and it is fine if the honest answer is that it depends.

[note] Confirm with the right professional before you commit to a route, especially on authority, disclosure, liens and tax.

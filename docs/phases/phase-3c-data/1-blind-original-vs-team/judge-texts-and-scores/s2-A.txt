## Who has to sign, and who else is affected?

The person who holds title, or who has the legal authority to sell for the owner, has to sign. Depending on the situation, that might be an owner, a trustee, an executor, an administrator, a surviving spouse, or several heirs agreeing together. Others are affected too: the person living in the house, co-owners and heirs, any tenant or other occupant, and neighbors.

Before cleanout, before price, before anything else, I ask one question. Who actually holds title to the house?

It sounds like paperwork. It is really the whole sale. The answer decides who can sign, and who has to be involved. With a packed house, the person living there is often not the person on title. The home may have been a parent's or a relative's, and the family may be sorting out who decides while the house sits full.

### Start with who holds title

Title can sit with an individual, a trust, an estate or an LLC. Each one changes who has to sign and who has to agree. You may not know which one applies yet. That is normal, and it is the first thing to find out.

### Then ask who has the authority to sell

Holding the keys is not the same as having the authority to sign. When the person living in the house is not the person with authority, the sale can stall.

I am not going to tell you which rules apply to your family. That depends on the documents and the facts, and it is a question for a real estate attorney or a title company. If the house was inherited, [find out first whether you can legally sell it](https://www.twinhomebuyer.com/inherited-property-bay-area-legal-authority-to-sell/). A trust, a will, a probate case or several heirs can all change the process.

### Then look at who else is affected

A packed or hoarded house is often tied to family. The seller may be an adult child, an heir or a relative. The person living there may be reluctant to leave, unwell, or not ready. Treat that person with respect. Many families do not need to be judged. They need time, clarity and a practical way forward.

Who | Why they matter
The person living in the home | Their health, wishes and timing shape what is realistic. They may or may not hold title.
Co-owners and heirs | If more than one person has a say, they may all need to agree before anyone can move ahead.
A tenant or other occupant | Anyone else living there affects access, timing and what you can promise a buyer.
Neighbors | They may already be worried about the property, which can matter if a local office gets involved.
A professional organizer or cleanout crew | They can help the family sort, keep and clear, and they need clear direction from whoever has authority.

If you are handling a parent's home, [this guide on selling a house full of belongings](https://www.twinhomebuyer.com/sell-bay-area-house-full-of-belongings/) speaks to that situation directly.

[callout] Settle who holds title and who can sign before you spend money on cleanout or talk price with anyone. Getting clarity early usually creates more options, not fewer.

[note] Confirm with a real estate attorney or a title company who holds title, who has authority to sell, and who must sign. This guide does not state probate or trust rules.

Q1. Do I have to disclose the condition of a packed or hoarded house when I sell in California?
A1. California's disclosure article applies to a sale of single-family residential property, with an exception. The disclosures are made on the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form, and any waiver of the requirements is void. Ask an agent or attorney how to complete it for your house.

Q2. Do I have to clean out a house before selling it?
A2. It depends on your time, your money, who has authority to sell and the route you pick. Clearing out shows the real condition before you list. A direct buyer prices in the condition. A city or county notice could also change your options.

Q3. Can the city or county act on a packed or hoarded house?
A3. It can look at the property. State law lists conditions that can make a building substandard, such as junk and debris that are fire, health or safety hazards. Whether a home meets them is for the local authority. Ask the city or county whether any notice is open.

Q4. Who can sell the house if the owner cannot or will not?
A4. That depends on who holds title and who has legal authority to sign. A sale can stall when the person living in the house is not that person. Confirm it with an attorney or title company before you plan anything else.

Q5. Can I agree with the buyer to skip disclosure?
A5. No, not for a sale the disclosure rules cover. Under California law, any waiver of the requirements is void as against public policy. Talk it through with your attorney before you sign anything.

Q6. What are my realistic options for a packed or hoarded house?
A6. It depends on what you need most. Keep it while the family sorts out care and decisions. Clear it out and list it if you have time and money. Sell directly to skip cleanout and showings, at a price that reflects condition. Confirm authority and title with an attorney or title company, tax with a CPA.

## What do you have to disclose when you sell a house in California?

In California, the disclosure rules in Civil Code 1102 apply, with an exception, to a transfer by sale of single-family residential property. The disclosures those rules require are made on a copy of the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form.

When the house is full, this is often the question that makes people freeze. Many owners think the clutter has to be explained away, or that it might be better not to mention it at all. Let me separate what the law says from what I can't tell you.

This is the part that matters most. Any waiver of the requirements of these disclosure rules is void as against public policy. In plain words, you can't make the disclosure requirement go away by agreement. A buyer saying they do not need the paperwork does not change it.

### What this means for a packed house

- Do not plan around skipping or hiding disclosure. The waiver rule means that route is not open.
- The exception matters. Whether your sale falls inside it, or whether your property counts as single-family residential, is a question for a professional, not a guess.
- This guide does not go through what the form asks. What belongs on it for your house is for your agent or attorney to walk through with you.

Clutter makes this harder because you may not know what is behind it. That is one reason people look at cleanout and inspection before listing. I cover that choice in the next section.

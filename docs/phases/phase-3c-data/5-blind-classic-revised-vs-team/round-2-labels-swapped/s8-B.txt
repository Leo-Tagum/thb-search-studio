## Who should you check with before you decide?

Before you pick a route, I'd check with an attorney, a title company, a CPA, an insurance professional and your city or county. Not all of them, and not all at once. But each can settle a question that this guide can only point at. A house full of belongings is hard enough without finding out halfway through that nobody checked who could sign.

- **Real estate attorney:** who has authority to sell, any probate or trust questions, and what has to be disclosed on the Transfer Disclosure Statement.
- **Listing agent:** how the disclosure form is completed for your specific home, if you are thinking about listing.
- **Title company:** liens, title issues and who has to sign.
- **CPA:** the tax consequences of selling.
- **Insurance professional:** what coverage looks like on a vacant or hazardous property.
- **City or county building, health or fire department:** whether any enforcement notice or local rule applies to the property.

If a notice has already been posted or a letter has come, start with the city or county. It is better to know what is open before you choose a path than to hear about it from a buyer.

[note] Confirm legal, tax, title, code and insurance points with the right professional for your situation. This guide explains the questions to ask. It does not replace the answers.

Once you know who can sign, what is owed and what the city or county says, you can compare keeping it, clearing it, listing it and selling directly with real information.

If a direct sale is one of the routes you want to talk through, the next step is the [packed and hoarded house page](https://www.twinhomebuyer.com/hoarder-houses/). Read it, then decide. There is no rush attached to it.

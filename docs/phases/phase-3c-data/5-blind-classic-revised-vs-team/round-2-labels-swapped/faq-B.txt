Q1. Do I have to disclose the condition of a packed or hoarded house when I sell in California?
A1. Generally yes, for a sale of single-family residential property. California's disclosure article applies, with an exception. The disclosures are made on the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form, and any waiver of the requirements is void. Go through the form with an agent or attorney before you list.

Q2. Do I have to clean out a house before selling it?
A2. Not always. It depends on your time, your money, who has authority to sell and which route you pick. Clearing it out shows the real condition before you list. A direct sale is the other route, and a direct buyer prices in the condition.

Q3. Can the city or county act on a packed or hoarded house?
A3. Possibly. State law lists conditions that can make a building substandard, such as junk and debris that are fire, health or safety hazards. Whether a home meets them is for the local authority. The statute does not mention hoarding. Ask the city or county whether any notice is open.

Q4. Who can sell the house if the owner cannot or will not?
A4. Only someone with legal authority to sign. That might be the owner, a trustee, an executor or administrator, or several heirs who agree. Living in the house alone does not give you that authority. Confirm who can sign with an attorney or title company before you plan anything else.

Q5. Can I agree with the buyer to skip disclosure?
A5. No, not for a sale the disclosure article covers. Under the California statute, any waiver of the disclosure requirements is void as against public policy. Work through the form with an agent or attorney instead.

Q6. What are my realistic options for a packed or hoarded house?
A6. It depends on your time, your money and who can sign. Keep it while you sort out care and decisions. Clear it out, then list it with an agent. Get professional help first on authority, title and tax. Or sell directly if you want to avoid cleanout and showings, accepting that the price reflects the condition.

## What do you have to disclose when you sell a house in California?

With a full house, the worry is that you do not know what you have to tell a buyer about its state, and telling them feels like it could hurt the sale. When you sell single-family residential property in California, the disclosures are made on the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form, the rules have an exception, and a waiver of them is void. Here is what that means, and what I would do about the rest.

- California's seller disclosure law (Civil Code 1102 and following) applies, except as provided in Section 1102.2, to a transfer by sale of single-family residential property.
- Under Civil Code 1102.6(a), the disclosures that law requires are made on a copy of the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form.
- Under Civil Code 1102(c), any waiver of the requirements of California's seller disclosure law is void as against public policy.

### What this means for a packed house

The waiver point matters most when the house is hard to face. You cannot get out of the requirements by agreeing with a buyer to skip them. So the useful question is not whether to disclose. It is how to do it accurately, with help.

- The rules have an exception. Whether your sale falls inside it, or whether your property counts as single-family residential, is a question to settle with a professional.
- This guide does not go through what the form asks. What belongs on it for your house is for your agent or attorney to walk through with you.
- Clutter can hide conditions you do not know about yet. That is one reason to think about cleanout and an inspection before you list. The next section covers that choice.

### What I would do first

1. Write down what you actually know about the house: rooms you cannot get into, damage you have seen, anything a neighbor or official has mentioned.
2. Take that list to a real estate attorney or a listing agent before you talk price with anyone.
3. Ask them to tell you whether the rules and the exception apply to your sale, and to go through the form with you for your specific house.
4. Decide on cleanout, listing or a direct sale after that, not before.

[note] Not sure who to call? Start with a real estate attorney for the legal questions, or your listing agent for how the form is filled out.

## What should you know first about a packed or hoarded house before you sell?

[answer capsule] You can sell a hoarded house in California. Which route fits depends on who has authority, the condition, the people involved, what is owed and how soon you need it done. Disclosure still applies: the law covers a sale of single-family residential property, with exceptions, and a waiver of its requirements is void.

The house is full. Maybe it is your parent's home, maybe a relative's, maybe your own. Every room is packed, and the idea of selling feels impossible. You do not know whether to clear it out first, and you do not know what you are required to tell a buyer.

A house this full carries history, and if someone is still living there, every decision touches them. That is a family matter before it is a real estate matter. Plan with the person who lives there, not around them, and keep judgment out of it.

How fast the house can be emptied is the question that feels urgent. The one that decides things is what situation you are in. I'd want these answered first, because each one changes what you can do:

1. **Who has authority?** Who holds title, and who is allowed to sign? If the person living in the house is not that person, the sale can stall. An unsigned co-owner can hold up the whole plan.
2. **What is the condition?** What is in the house, whether any rooms or exits are blocked, and whether there may be damage hidden behind the belongings. A blocked exit changes what has to happen before anyone can safely walk through.
3. **Who is affected?** The occupant, co-owners, heirs, anyone else who lives there. All of them matter to the plan.
4. **What is owed?** Loans, back taxes, liens and unpaid bills, set against the cost of cleanout and repairs and what the house can sell for.
5. **How soon do you need this done?** Whether speed, certainty, privacy or the highest price matters most to you.

Your answers point to a path, and each path trades something. Keeping the home for now buys time and keeps control, but the problems wait for you. Clearing it out and listing it can reach the whole market, but it costs time and money up front. Professional help adds certainty on legal points, at the price of fees and time. Selling directly can cut the work, but a direct buyer prices in the condition, so you give up some price for speed and privacy.

The disclosure rules in the capsule above do not change with your answers. A sale of single-family residential property uses the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form, so plan to disclose, not to skip it. What goes on the form is a question for your agent or attorney, and a later section covers it.

[note] Authority, title, liens and disclosure are legal questions. Confirm them with a real estate attorney or a title company before you commit to a path. The next section starts with the first of the five: who has to sign.

## Do you have to clean out the house before you sell it?

Clearing the house out is your choice, and it does not change the disclosure rules if your sale is covered by them. The harder question is whether you can start at all. When every room is full, the first step can feel out of reach. That does not mean the house cannot be sold.

### What a cleanout does change

- It shows the condition. Once the rooms are clear, you and any buyer can see the floors, walls and ceilings that were covered by belongings.
- It gives you more to decide with. A professional cleanout crew, then an inspection, can show you more of what you are selling before you pick a route. An inspection still has limits, so ask the inspector what it did not cover.
- It makes showing the home possible, which matters if you want to market it broadly.

### What a cleanout does not change

A cleanout does not replace the disclosure form. You still complete it, if your sale is covered.

Here is the law in plain terms. The disclosure rules apply to a sale of single-family residential property, except as the law provides. The disclosures go on a copy of the Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement form. A waiver of the requirements is void as against public policy. So do not agree with anyone to skip the form.

I am not telling you what must go on the form for your house, or whether an exception fits your sale. Go through it with a listing agent or a real estate attorney.

### When you might choose not to clear it first

- You do not have the time, health or help to run a cleanout and the repairs that may follow.
- The person living there is not ready to move, and a rushed cleanout would hurt them more than it helps the sale.
- The family has not settled who decides. Clearing out belongings before that is settled can start a fight that is hard to undo.
- You want privacy and fewer showings, and you accept that a direct buyer will price in the condition. You would be trading some price for privacy and fewer showings.

None of those is a wrong answer. Each one trades something: time, price, control or certainty.

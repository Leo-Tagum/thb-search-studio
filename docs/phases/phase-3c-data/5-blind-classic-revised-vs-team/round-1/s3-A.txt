## What conditions can turn a cluttered house into a code or safety problem?

A full house is not automatically a code problem. But clutter can change character when it becomes trash, pests or fuel for a fire. The state statute lists conditions like those, and an official decides whether a home meets them.

California's Health and Safety Code, section 17920.3, lists conditions that can make a building substandard. I'd read them before you decide anything, so you know what the city or county can actually look at. The statute does not mention hoarding.

What it looks like | What the statute says | Who decides
Junk, debris and garbage piling up | An accumulation of junk, debris, garbage and similar materials that constitutes fire, health or safety hazards is among the listed conditions that can make a building substandard. | The local enforcement authority
Insects, vermin or rodents | Infestation of insects, vermin or rodents, as determined by a health officer, is listed as a condition of inadequate sanitation. | A health officer
No adequate way to store and remove garbage | Lack of adequate garbage and rubbish storage and removal facilities, as determined by a health officer, is listed as a condition of inadequate sanitation. | A health officer
Combustible waste or vegetation | Listed when, in the opinion of the fire chief or the chief's deputy, it is in a condition to cause a fire or explosion or provide ready fuel to spread fire. | The fire chief or the chief's deputy

Look at the last column. Whether a particular house meets any of these is not for you, me or a buyer to say. The local enforcement authority makes that call, and for several of these conditions the statute names a health officer or a fire official.

### What should you check before you pick a route?

- A pile of belongings is one thing. Trash or signs of pests are another, and they can resemble conditions the statute lists. Whether a home meets them is for the official to decide.
- Blocked rooms and hidden damage are practical concerns of their own. A buyer's inspection may find problems behind the clutter, so you will want to know about them first.
- Whether a notice or violation already exists on the property. Find that out before you choose how to sell, not in the middle of a sale.

Treat it as secondary to checking with your own city or county, because local rules differ. What you must tell a buyer is a separate question, and the next section covers it.
